微软确认 Outlook.com 退信故障并已回滚：11 月 6 日至 9 日发往 Outlook.com 等邮箱的邮件被误拦

正文 · AI 翻译

过去几天，Reddit 与 Microsoft Learn 上有大量报告称，发送到 Outlook.com、Hotmail、Live 与 MSN 地址的邮件因 SPF/DKIM/DMARC 认证错误（AS9031519）被退回。有意思的是，只有部分邮件被退回，同一封邮件被退回后，用户几分钟后重发又能正常投递。起初怀疑是缺少 DMARC 记录，但微软现已确认这是正在发生的 Exchange 服务问题。

更新：2026 年 11 月 10 日，微软已在 EX1545193 事件下解决该问题。根本原因是垃圾邮件 enforcement 规则的一次更新，错误地将发往 Outlook.com、Hotmail 与 Live 地址的邮件标记并拦截。微软已回滚该更新，邮件流于 11 月 9 日 12:02（中欧时间）恢复。如果用户在该时间之后仍看到 AS9031519 退信，检查邮件跟踪并开支持工单。

退信来自微软的消费者邮件服务器（*.olc.protection.outlook.com 域名之后），因此问题只影响消费者邮箱，发往 Exchange Online 企业邮箱的邮件正常投递。发送方不限于 Microsoft 365：Neowin 报道称 Google 与 Zoho 的发件方同样被拒，Reddit 上的管理员称本地 Exchange 服务器发出的邮件也一样。

令人困惑的是退信通知：用户被告知 hotmail.com 无法确认邮件来自可信位置，状态码为 550 5.7.23，附 SPF 验证错误。这只是外层包装，真正的原因在退信详情或邮件跟踪中："550 5.7.1 Message rejected: Message lacks SPF/DKIM/DMARC Auth Records (AS9031519)"。首次退信出现于 11 月 5 日。退信通知把问题指向 SPF 记录，但多数情况下 SPF 并无问题，修改 SPF 之前先确认 AS9031519 这一原因码。退信是间歇性的：一封邮件被退回，15 分钟后重发又投递了；或同一域名的某位用户被拒，而同事发往同一 Outlook.com 地址却没有问题。因此单次测试邮件成功几乎说明不了什么。

是 DMARC 的问题还是微软的问题？微软已确认是其一侧的服务问题，但缺少 DMARC 记录也可能起了作用。微软确实有针对 Outlook.com 的公开规则，要求发往 Outlook.com 的发件方具备 SPF、DKIM 与 DMARC，适用于 2025 年 5 月起每天发送超过 5,000 封邮件的高量发件方。但该规则对应的是另一错误码"550 5.7.515 Access denied"，且多数受影响域名每天发送量远不到 5,000 封；AS9031519 这一代码在微软任何文档中都查不到。不少受影响域名没有 DMARC 记录，Reddit r/sysadmin 板块许多管理员报告称发布 DMARC 记录后退信停止。但另一方面，Microsoft Q&A 板块也有报告称，有些域名多年来一直配置了 SPF、DKIM 与 DMARC，邮件头中 spf=pass、dkim=pass、dmarc=pass，邮件仍时不时被拒，缺少 DMARC 记录解释不了这些情况。11 月 9 日，一位微软版主在同一 Q&A 帖子中确认，发往 Outlook.com、Hotmail 与 Live 地址的邮件存在正在发生的 Exchange 服务问题，公告定级为服务降级，微软仍在调查根本原因。根据事件报告（EX1545193），近期一次垃圾邮件 enforcement 规则更新导致合法邮件被标记并拦截，微软已回滚，邮件流于 11 月 9 日恢复。两个细节值得注意：微软称故障始于 11 月 6 日 19:00（中欧时间），而首次报告出现于 11 月 5 日；事件编号是 EX1545193，不是 Reddit 上提到的 EX1540236。另请注意，退信通知把 SPF 展开为"Service Provider Foundation"，对运行邮件过滤的团队而言不是什么信心提振。为什么添加 DMARC 能让许多域名的退信停止，报告中没有解释。作者推测，故障规则对缺失认证记录的权重更高，但这只是推测。

无论结果如何，发件域名具备 SPF、DKIM 与 DMARC 记录总归是好的，添加只需几分钟，值得作为变通办法。作者给出一段 PowerShell 脚本，从 Exchange Online 获取全部已接受域，用公共 DNS 解析器检查各域的 SPF、DKIM 与 DMARC 记录，并导出为 CSV：需要 ExchangeOnlineManagement 模块，Resolve-DnsName 仅在 Windows 上可用。需要优先修复的域名是 DMARC 为 False 的域名；SPF 记录必须恰好为 1 条，两个 SPF 记录的域名 SPF 完全失效；若 DKIM 已启用但 CNAME 不匹配，说明 DNS 中的 CNAME 记录与 Exchange Online 期望的选择器不一致。若还通过第三方服务（新闻通讯工具、CRM、扫描仪中继）发邮件，仅检查 SPF 是否包含 Exchange Online 只能看到一半，那些服务也须列入 SPF 记录，或用发件域名做 DKIM 签名。

配置 SPF、DKIM 与 DMARC：SPF 为根域上的一条 TXT 记录，包含 Exchange Online 与所有其他代发服务，例如 v=spf1 include:spf.protection.outlook.com -all。DKIM：Microsoft 365 在为自定义域启用 DKIM 之前，用 onmicrosoft.com 域名签名外发邮件，该签名与 From 地址中的域名不匹配，因此不计入 DMARC。步骤为打开 Microsoft Defender 门户，进入电子邮件与协作 > 策略与规则 > 威胁策略 > 电子邮件身份验证设置，打开 DKIM 选项卡并选择域名，向 DNS 添加两条 CNAME 记录，解析生效后启用"使用 DKIM 签名对此域的消息"。DMARC：若域名尚无 DMARC 记录，从监控策略起步：TXT 记录，名称/主机为 _dmarc，值为 v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:dmarc@yourdomain.com。p=none 时接收方不对未通过验证的邮件采取行动，只发送报告，因此不会改变 Gmail 或其他方对待邮件的方式。报告干净后可升级到 quarantine 或 reject。注意主机名必须是 _dmarc，不是 @。一些管理员按通用教程把记录加到根域，退信持续，直到移到 _dmarc 才停止；部分 DNS 服务商要求完整名称 _dmarc.yourdomain.com，按服务商要求填写。更改 DNS 后给生效留出时间，一些管理员在添加记录后几小时内仍看到退信；由于退信是间歇性的，用分散在一天中的多封测试邮件验证，不要只发一封。

仍被拒怎么办：若域名通过 SPF、DKIM 与 DMARC 检查，邮件仍以 AS9031519 被拒，问题在微软一侧，能做的事不多，但可确保问题被上报：1）在 Exchange 管理中心进入邮件流 > 邮件跟踪，找到失败邮件并打开详情，确认原因为 AS9031519 而非其他错误；2）在 Microsoft 365 管理中心开支持工单，附上邮件跟踪中的最新网络邮件 ID 与几封受影响邮件的 .eml 文件，这是微软要求纳入调查的材料；若订阅通过合作伙伴购买，须由合作伙伴开工单；3）向微软 Outlook.com 发件方支持报告；4）若收件人是合作客户或联系人，对方也可从自己的邮箱报告，在 Outlook 网页版中为帮助 > 反馈 > 报告问题。并告知用户：提到 SPF 的退信会让人以为自己的邮件坏了，简短说明问题在 Outlook.com 一侧、重发通常可行，可为服务台省去几通电话。

总结：要求所有发往 Outlook.com 的发件方都具备 DMARC 并非坏事，Google 与 Yahoo 自 2024 年起已要求批量发件方具备 DMARC，没有 DMARC 的域名很容易被仿冒。难的是判断修复是否真的生效：由于只有部分邮件被拒，单次测试邮件成功证明不了什么。因此运行脚本、补齐缺失的 DMARC 与 DKIM，并留意接下来几天的邮件跟踪。

原文

There are a lot of reports last couple of days on Reddit and Microsoft Learn about emails sent to Outlook.com, Hotmail, Live and MSN addresses that are bouncing with an SPF/DKIM/DMARC Auth error (AS9031519).

Now the interesting part is that only a part of the emails are bouncing to those domains. The same message can bounce and then go through fine when the user sends it again a few minutes later. Missing DMARC records where the supposed issue, but Microsoft has now confirmed it's an ongoing Exchange service issue.

Update November 10, 2026 - Microsoft has resolved the issue under EX1545193. The root cause was an update to the spam enforcement rules that incorrectly marked and blocked messages to Outlook.com, Hotmail and Live addresses. Microsoft rolled the update back, and mail flow was restored on November 9 at 12:02 CET. If your users still see AS9031519 bounces after that time, check the message trace and open a support case.

The rejection comes from Microsoft's consumer mail servers, the ones behind *.olc.protection.outlook.com, so the issue only affects consumer mailboxes. Mail to business mailboxes in Exchange Online are delivered as normal.

The sending side isn't limited to Microsoft 365. Neowin reported senders on Google and Zoho getting rejected as well, and admins on Reddit see the same from on-premises Exchange servers.

What makes it confusing is the NDR. The user is told that hotmail.com couldn't confirm the message was sent from a trusted location, with status code 550 5.7.23 and an SPF validation error. That's only the wrapper. In the error details of the NDR, or in the message trace, you will find the actual reason: "550 5.7.1 Message rejected: Message lacks SPF/DKIM/DMARC Auth Records (AS9031519)".

The first rejections where noticed on November 5.

The NDR points you to your SPF record, but in most cases there is nothing wrong with SPF. Look for the AS9031519 reason before you start changing your SPF record. And the rejections are intermittent. A message bounces, the user resends it 15 minutes later and it's delivered. Or one user gets rejected while a colleague in the same domain sends to the same Outlook.com address without any problem. So a single successful test email tells you pretty much nothing.

Microsoft has confirmed it's a service issue on their side. But a missing DMARC record might play a part as well. Microsoft does have a published rule that requires SPF, DKIM and DMARC for Outlook.com: the requirements for high-volume senders, which apply to domains sending more than 5,000 messages a day since May 2025. But that rule comes with its own error, "550 5.7.515 Access denied", and most of the affected domains send nowhere near 5,000 messages a day. The AS9031519 code isn't documented anywhere by Microsoft.

What I do read is that a lot of the affected domains don't have a DMARC record. Many admins in the r/sysadmin thread report that the bounces stopped after they published one. But on the other hand, there are also reports in the Microsoft Q&A thread of domains that have had SPF, DKIM and DMARC in place for years, with spf=pass, dkim=pass and dmarc=pass in the message headers, and their mail still gets rejected now and then. A missing DMARC record doesn't explain those.

On November 9, a Microsoft moderator confirmed in that same Q&A thread that there is an ongoing Exchange service issue with messages to Outlook.com, Hotmail and Live addresses. The advisory is classified as a service degradation, and Microsoft is still investigating the root cause.

So it was a Microsoft issue after all. According to the incident report (EX1545193), a recent update to the spam enforcement rules caused legitimate messages to be marked and blocked. Microsoft rolled it back, and mail flow was restored on November 9.

Two details stand out. Microsoft puts the start at November 6, 19:00 CET, while the first reports came in on November 5. And the incident ID isn't the EX1540236 that was mentioned on Reddit. Also note that the NDR expands SPF as "Service Provider Foundation".

Why adding DMARC stopped the bounces for so many domains isn't explained in the report. The author's guess is that the faulty rule weighed missing authentication records more heavily, but that's speculation.

Now whatever the outcome is, it's always good to have the SPF, DKIM and DMARC records in place for your domain. Adding them only takes a couple of minutes, so it's worth a short as workaround. The author provides a PowerShell script that gets all accepted domains from Exchange Online and checks the SPF, DKIM and DMARC records in public DNS (using the ExchangeOnlineManagement module; Resolve-DnsName only works on Windows) and exports the result as a CSV. A domain where Dmarc is False is the first thing to fix. SpfRecords should be exactly 1, because a domain with two SPF records fails SPF completely. And if DkimEnabled is True but DkimCnameOK is False, the CNAME records in DNS don't match the selectors from Exchange Online. If you also send mail through a third-party service (a newsletter tool, a CRM, a scanner relay), SpfIncludesEXO only tells you half of it. Those services need to be in your SPF record too, or sign with DKIM for your domain.

Configure SPF, DKIM and DMARC anyway: SPF is one TXT record on the root domain that includes Exchange Online and every other service that sends mail for your domain, for example "v=spf1 include:spf.protection.outlook.com -all". For DKIM, Microsoft 365 signs outgoing mail with your onmicrosoft.com domain until you enable DKIM for your custom domain. That signature doesn't match the domain in the From address, so it doesn't count for DMARC. Steps: open the Microsoft Defender portal, go to Email & collaboration > Policies & rules > Threat policies > Email authentication settings, open the DKIM tab and select your domain, add the two CNAME records to your DNS, and enable "Sign messages for this domain with DKIM signatures" once they resolve. For DMARC, if your domain has no DMARC record yet, start with a monitoring policy: TXT record, name/host "_dmarc", value "v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:dmarc@yourdomain.com". With p=none, receivers don't take any action on failed messages, they only send you reports. Once the reports look clean, you can move to quarantine or reject. Note the host name has to be "_dmarc", not "@". Give it some time after changing DNS; some admins saw bounces for a few more hours after adding the record. And because the rejections are intermittent, test with a few messages spread over the day, not a single one.

Still rejected? If your domain passes SPF, DKIM and DMARC and messages still bounce with AS9031519, the problem is on Microsoft's side. Confirm the reason in the message trace (Exchange admin center > Mail flow > Message trace), open a support case in the Microsoft 365 admin center with fresh Network Message IDs from the message trace and a few affected messages saved as .eml files, and report it to Microsoft's Outlook.com sender support. And tell your users: a bounce that mentions SPF makes people think their email is broken. A short message that the problem is on the Outlook.com side, and that resending usually works, saves your helpdesk a few calls.

Requiring DMARC from everyone who sends to Outlook.com isn't a bad move. Google and Yahoo have required it from bulk senders since 2024, and a domain without DMARC is pretty easy to spoof. The hard part is knowing whether your fix actually worked. Because only some messages are rejected, one successful test email doesn't prove much. So run the script, add DMARC and DKIM where they're missing, and keep an eye on the message trace for the next few days.
