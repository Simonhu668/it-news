Ignite 2026 后微软 AI 计费变了：座位价不变，Copilot Credits 按任务计费，2027 年初起 Copilot Business 默认开启用量计费

官方依据：微软 Ignite 2026（11 月 17 日）公告，经 Greyhound Research 11 月 19 日分析｜适用产品及版本：Microsoft 365 Copilot（30 美元/用户/月）、Agent 365（15…

正文 · AI 翻译

微软在 2026 年 11 月 17 日的 Ignite 大会上没有调整座位价，但扩大了座位旁边的计量器。Microsoft 365 Copilot 仍为每用户每月 30 美元，Agent 365 为 15 美元，Microsoft 365 E7 为 99 美元。Copilot Credits 是微软的用量计费货币，现在还可支付 Windows 365 for Agents、Fabric 的新计费选项，以及 Outlook 与 Copilot 应用中的 Today Feed。Agent 365 Cost Management 与 Business Analytics（微软称之为 FinOps for AI）用于呈现与控制这笔支出。多数新功能处于预览或 Frontier 早期访问计划，部分积分费率尚未公布。

Greyhound Research 的判断是：可预测性已从微软的价格表转移到客户自己写下的支出策略。座位每月固定；积分购买的是一项任务，其价格是一个区间，取自全公司共享的池子。决定谁可以花、花多少、在哪些服务上花的策略，如今成为财务团队拥有的控制手段。微软公布的计量器比竞争对手更完整，剩余的大部分不确定性来自买方可以控制的设置与习惯。

三项发现支撑这一论点。第一，Copilot Credits 是一种货币，但还不是一张账单：部分服务在 Microsoft 365 管理中心配置，另一些在 Power Platform 管理中心或 Azure 订阅中配置。第二，支出上限是天花板，不是预算：它不预留任何额度，触顶后即停止该用户当月的工作，且默认情况下每个新上线的计量服务都会加入已有策略。对于通过微软合作伙伴购买的 Copilot Business，微软计划自 2027 年初起默认开启用量计费本身，上限为每用户每月 4,000 积分。第三，微软现在既计量支出，也对价值打分，因此企业应保留自己对工作价值的衡量。

座位价格未变的另一面是组织影响：三十年来，微软预算按座位数乘以标价编制，年度 true-up 结算差额；计量计费的账单每月都在动，推动它的是用户而非采购团队。微软的文档已预示未来一年将有更多服务加入计量器，AI 预算中因此出现了一条不受任何一次合同签署控制的费用线。

已公布的价格与费率（截至 2026 年 11 月 19 日）：Microsoft 365 Copilot 座位每用户每月 30 美元（按年支付，包含于 Microsoft 365 E7）；Agent 365 每用户每月 15 美元（按年支付，自 2026 年 5 月 1 日起）；Microsoft 365 E7 每用户每月 99 美元（含 Teams）、不含 Teams 为 90.45 美元（自 2026 年 5 月 1 日起，是否包含 Copilot Credits 尚未明确）；Copilot Credits 按量计费每积分 0.01 美元，月后结算，预付 P3 计划按量有 5% 至 20% 折扣。未公布的包括：Today Feed、Fabric 与 Windows 365 for Agents 的积分费率，以及期限内的费率保护。

许可门槛方面，Ignite 在边缘放宽了两处许可门槛，但主要门槛未动：企业级代理治理与对其他公司代理的洞察仍须 Agent 365 或 E7。续约谈判的杠杆也在变化：座位按年以标价续订，预付积分提前一年购买、未用完即过期，且微软称费率可能调整，因此谈判重点应放在积分条款上，而不仅是座位价。

Greyhound 的十步 CXO 行动清单中，前三步可直接表述：关闭新服务的自动覆盖、按业务单元各写一份策略；把积分预算按区间编制；为每个关键流程预先决定触顶后如何处理，再上线。其余步骤覆盖 FinOps 负责人、董事会成员、CIO、采购、业务负责人与架构师的职责。证据的局限在于：多项积分费率尚未公布，包括 Today Feed、Fabric 与 Windows 365 for Agents；微软的许可指南称所有价格可能变更。在这些信号到来之前，建议仍然成立：按实测用量购买预付积分，并在续约时像谈座位价一样认真谈积分条款。

原文

At Microsoft Ignite on November 17, 2026, Microsoft left its seat prices where they were and widened the meter beside them. Microsoft 365 Copilot stays at USD 30 per user a month, Agent 365 at USD 15, and Microsoft 365 E7 at USD 99. Copilot Credits, Microsoft's usage-based currency, now also pays for Windows 365 for Agents, a new billing option in Fabric, and the Today Feed in Outlook and the Copilot app. Agent 365 Cost Management and Business Analytics, which Microsoft calls FinOps for AI, are meant to show and control that spend. Most of the new pieces are in preview or the Frontier early-access program.

Greyhound Research's position is that predictability has moved from Microsoft's price list to the spending policy the customer writes. A seat costs the same every month. A credit buys a task whose price is a range, drawn from a pool shared across the whole company. The policy that decides who may spend, how much, and on which services is now the control a finance team owns. Greyhound Research reads this as a familiar stage in any move to metered pricing, and a manageable one. Microsoft has published more of its meter than its rivals have, and most of the variance that remains comes from settings and habits the buyer controls.

Three findings carry the argument. First, Copilot Credits are one currency but not yet one bill: some services are set up in the Microsoft 365 admin center, others in the Power Platform admin center or an Azure subscription. Second, a spending limit is a ceiling, not a budget. It reserves nothing, it stops the user's work for the rest of the month when it is reached, and by default every new metered service joins existing policies. For Copilot Business bought through Microsoft's partners, Microsoft plans to switch usage-based billing itself on by default from early 2027, with a limit of 4,000 credits per user a month. Third, Microsoft now meters the spend and also scores the value, so the business should keep its own measure of what the work was worth.

Microsoft announced no new price for its seats at Ignite. A seat is a license for one named person, paid every month at a fixed price. Microsoft 365 Copilot is listed at USD 30 per user a month, Agent 365 at USD 15, and Microsoft 365 E7, which bundles both with Microsoft 365 E5 and more security tools, at USD 99 on its plans page. What changed is the meter beside the seats. Holding list prices while widening the meter is a common move in enterprise software, and it draws attention away from the number buyers watch most closely. For three decades, Microsoft budgets have been built on seat counts multiplied by list prices, with the annual true-up settling the difference. A metered line does not wait for the true-up. It moves every month, and the people who move it are users, not the procurement team. Greyhound Research expects more Microsoft services to join the meter over the coming year, as Microsoft's documentation already anticipates. The effect most coverage misses is organizational. The AI budget now carries a line that no single contract signature controls.

Published prices and rates (as of November 19, 2026): Microsoft 365 Copilot seat — USD 30 per user a month, paid yearly, included in Microsoft 365 E7; Agent 365 — USD 15 per user a month, paid yearly, since May 1, 2026; Microsoft 365 E7 — USD 99 per user a month with Teams, USD 90.45 without, since May 1, 2026, whether any Copilot Credits are included is not yet published; Copilot Credits, pay-as-you-go — USD 0.01 per credit, billed after the month, prepaid P3 plan 5% to 20% off by volume. Not yet published: rates for the Today Feed, Fabric, and Windows 365 for Agents, and rate protection for a term. The evidence has limits. Several credit rates are not yet published, including those for the Today Feed, Fabric, and Windows 365 for Agents. Microsoft's licensing guide says all pricing is subject to change. Until they arrive, the advice holds: buy prepaid credits from measured use, and negotiate the terms for credits at renewal as hard as the price of seats.
