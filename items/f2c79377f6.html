Oracle E-Business Suite 12.2 时区补丁 DST v47 发布：覆盖曼尼托巴与西北地区 11 月 1 日起的固定时区

官方依据：Oracle E-Business Suite 技术博客 11 月 3 日公告，经独立博客转述｜适用产品及版本：Oracle E-Business Suite 12.2，时区补丁 39114624 仍适用｜生效日期：2026 年…

正文 · AI 翻译

Oracle 11 月 3 日宣布，DST v47 时区更新已获认证，可用于 E-Business Suite（EBS）12.2。此次更新跟进加拿大两个地区最近的时钟规则变化：西北地区与曼尼托巴省。两地均于 2026 年 11 月 1 日起改为固定偏移，不再回拨一小时；在这些时区记录交易时间的 EBS 站点，或与当地有业务往来的站点，需要检查补丁集。Oracle DST 补丁是累积的，v47 包含此前全部时区更新；上一版获 EBS 认证的是 v46。本次包含两项新规则变化，均取自上游时区数据版本：西北地区（America/Inuvik，TZDATA2026d）自 2026 年 11 月 1 日 02:00 当地时间起保持 UTC-06，缩写 CST，2026 年最后一次向前拨钟后不再回拨；曼尼托巴省（America/Winnipeg 与 Canada/Central，TZDATA2026e）2026 年 3 月 8 日的向前拨钟为可预见未来的最后一次时钟变化，自 2026 年 11 月 1 日 02:00 当地时间起保持 UTC-05，缩写 EST，Canada/Central 兼容链接同步跟随。西北地区其余部分使用 America/Edmonton，阿尔伯塔省的对应变化已包含在 TZDATA2026c 中；Oracle 补充，若安大略省西北部个别社区选择不跟随曼尼托巴，可能还会有后续时区版本。为什么应用套件会在意时钟规则？EBS 存储并展示业务事件的时间，错误的时区定义会让时间戳偏差一小时，对订单、发票、薪酬与审计记录而言，可能在业务与法律层面产生影响。Oracle 给出的需要打补丁的条件有两条：EBS 环境位于受影响国家或地区；或环境位于别处，但与受影响地区的客户或供应商有业务往来。多时区配置的 EBS 应通过此类补丁保持时区定义文件最新；不受影响地区的单时区站点工作量不大，但仍值得快速检查。完整流程见 My Oracle Support 文章 KA1761，即 EBS 12.2 遵守 DST 与时区规则变化的指南，可帮助判断堆栈中哪些部分需要更新并列出各补丁与步骤；Oracle 表示该说明取代较旧的 PNEWS2872。一个容易遗漏的细节：由于 v47 未新增时区，此前发布的 EBS 时区补丁 39114624 同样适用于本次更新，尚未应用的须一并应用。Oracle 同时说明了跳过的代价：未打补丁的受影响系统继续使用旧规则，部分交易时间可能被错误存储。时区更新属于常规工作，但容易被推迟，因为在跨越日期边界之前看不出明显故障；加拿大变化已于 11 月 1 日生效，受影响系统若未打补丁，实际上已在使用过时规则，因此这是近期任务而非留待下个维护窗口的事项。文章给出的可行计划是：列出 EBS 实例、所在区域以及用户与合作伙伴所在国家；阅读 KA1761 第 2 节，判断应用层、客户端或数据库层哪些层受影响；在非生产副本中测试补丁并对比几个已知时间戳的前后变化；在常规维护时段推广到生产环境并确认日期敏感任务表现正常。单独运行 Oracle 数据库的团队还须注意，数据库时区文件属于不同层次，有各自的更新路径。结论是：DST v47 使 EBS 12.2 与曼尼托巴与西北地区的固定时区决定保持一致，补丁累积且 39114624 仍适用；在当地运营或与当地有贸易往来的组织，应按 KA1761 尽快应用更新。

原文

Oracle announced on November 3, 2026 that the DST v47 timezone updates are certified and available for E-Business Suite (EBS) Release 12.2. The update follows recent changes to clock rules in two Canadian regions, the Northwest Territories and Manitoba. Both regions moved to a permanent offset on November 1, 2026, so they no longer fall back an hour. EBS sites that record transaction times in those zones, or deal with partners there, need to review the patch set. Oracle DST patches are cumulative, so v47 includes everything shipped in earlier timezone updates. The previous release certified with EBS was v46. This one carries two new rule changes, both taken from upstream timezone data releases. Northwest Territories (America/Inuvik, TZDATA2026d): from 02:00 local time on November 1, 2026 the zone stays on UTC-06 with the abbreviation CST. There is no fall-back after the last spring-forward of 2026. Manitoba (America/Winnipeg and Canada/Central, TZDATA2026e): the spring-forward on March 8, 2026 was the last clock change for the foreseeable future. From 02:00 local time on November 1, 2026 the province stays on UTC-05 with the abbreviation EST, and the Canada/Central compatibility link follows. The rest of the Northwest Territories uses America/Edmonton, and the matching change for Alberta was already part of TZDATA2026c. Oracle adds that a further timezone release could follow, should some northwestern Ontario communities choose not to match Manitoba. Why does an application suite care about a clock rule? EBS stores and shows times for business events, and a wrong timezone definition means those timestamps can be off by an hour. For orders, invoices, payroll and audit records, that can matter for business and legal reasons. Oracle says your environment needs patching if either of two conditions is true: the EBS environment is physically located in an affected country or region, or the environment is located elsewhere, but you do business with customers or suppliers in the affected region. If your EBS is set up to work across several time zones, you should keep its timezone definition files current through these patches. Single-zone sites in unaffected regions may have little to do, but a quick review is still worthwhile. The full procedure lives in My Oracle Support article KA1761, the EBS 12.2 guide on complying with DST and timezone rule changes. It helps you work out which parts of your stack need updates and lists each patch with steps. Oracle says this note replaces the older PNEWS2872. One detail is easy to miss: because v47 adds no brand new time zones, the EBS timezone patch 39114624 that was released earlier also applies to this update. If you have not applied it yet, you need to apply it now. Oracle also spells out the cost of skipping this: an affected system left unpatched keeps using the old rules, and some transaction times may be stored wrongly. Timezone updates are routine, but they are easy to postpone because nothing visibly breaks until a date boundary is crossed. The Canadian change took effect on November 1, so affected systems are already living with outdated rules unless they have been patched. A practical plan: list your EBS instances, their regions and the countries your users and partners work in; read Section 2 of KA1761 to decide which tiers (application, client or database) are affected; test the patches in a non-production copy and compare a few known timestamps before and after; roll the change to production during a normal maintenance slot and confirm date-sensitive jobs behave as expected. Teams that also run the Oracle Database separately should remember that database timezone files are a different layer with their own update path. DST v47 brings Oracle E-Business Suite 12.2 in line with the permanent-time decisions in Manitoba and the Northwest Territories. The patches are cumulative, and the existing timezone patch 39114624 still applies. If you operate in or trade with those regions, follow KA1761 and apply the update soon.
