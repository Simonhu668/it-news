原文

SonicWall has patched a SonicOS SSL-VPN flaw that lets someone with valid credentials skip SAML login controls, and it is now on CISA's list of exploited vulnerabilities. The flaw is CVE-2026-19868, and it gets the most attention because it can undermine the MFA you configured to stop credential theft. It isn't a "walk in the front door" bug. It is a case where an attacker can pick a weaker door that is still unlocked. CVE-2026-19868 lets attackers with valid credentials for an alternate authentication method bypass SAML authentication controls, potentially including SAML-enforced MFA. The CVE record classifies it as CWE-308, use of single-factor authentication. The scope is narrow. BleepingComputer reports that it only affects SonicOS SSL-VPN services configured to use SAML authentication, and it reports a CVSS score of 7.1. Other aggregators list 7.5, so treat 7.1 as SonicWall's rating and expect different numbers on third-party dashboards. SonicWall's advisory ID is SNWLID-2026-0014. It covers Gen 7 and Gen 8 firewalls, physical and virtual. Gen 7: SonicOS 7.3.3-7015 and older affected, fixed in 7.3.4-7013 and above. Gen 8: SonicOS 8.2.2-8015 and older affected, fixed in 8.2.3-8015. 7.3.4-7013 became available on October 20, 2026, and 7.3.4-7015 on November 5. 8.2.3-8015 was scheduled for November 6. Gen 6 and older firewalls have reached end-of-support and get no firmware updates. SonicWall tells those customers to move to Gen 8 hardware. BleepingComputer reports that SonicWall says no workarounds are available, so the fix is to upgrade. SonicWall's wording is careful. It said its PSIRT investigated a report of potential exploitation and urged customers to upgrade quickly. SonicWall hasn't published incident details, and BleepingComputer notes the company has not shared specifics. CISA is more definitive. CISA added the flaw to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, which confirms exploitation in the wild. The Canadian Centre for Cyber Security's advisory AV26-1133 also says SonicWall indicates the flaw is being exploited. It confirms CISA's KEV addition on November 6, 2026. CISA ordered federal civilian executive branch agencies to secure affected systems by November 20, 2026. That deadline is a federal directive. Private organizations aren't bound by it, but it is a useful benchmark for how quickly to move. So far there is no public information on who is exploiting this, how many devices are affected, or whether the attacks involved stolen credentials. The "valid credentials" requirement suggests a credential-theft angle, but that is an inference. The advisory also fixes other SonicOS problems: two management interface flaws rated 8.8 that let unauthenticated attackers perform unauthorized operations under specific conditions; pre-authentication buffer overflows in the SSL-VPN and TACACS+ services that could cause denial of service; and a weak password hashing flaw affecting virtual appliances. The KEV listing applies only to CVE-2026-19868, so the other flaws are not confirmed as exploited. What admins should do: inventory Gen 7 and Gen 8 firewalls and their SonicOS builds, prioritizing any with SAML enabled for SSL-VPN logins; upgrade Gen 7 to 7.3.4-7013 or later, and Gen 8 to 8.2.3-8015; review which alternate login methods are enabled next to SAML for SSL-VPN; check SSL-VPN logins that didn't come through the identity provider; plan for end-of-support devices, as Gen 6 and older hardware gets no patch. Edge devices keep turning up on exploited-vulnerability lists, and VPN authentication is an attractive target. A SAML bypass is a good reminder that adding MFA isn't enough. You also have to close every older login path that skips it.
