从计划到行动：用 Oracle 与 NVIDIA 搭建智能供应链的决策架构

作者提出 Planning Decision Fabric 架构：以 Oracle E-Business Suite、ASCP、Fusion 供应链计划与 Oracle AI Data Platform、Oracle AI Database…

medium·社区经验AI应用行业视野收录 11-16 18:30

正文 · AI 翻译

供应商的一批货延期四天。供应链计划员首先要问：哪些客户承诺现在面临风险？

答案分散在整个企业里：采购掌握供应商的最新确认，计划掌握预期供应，生产知道哪些工单需要这个部件，客户运营知道哪些承诺最重要，仓库与运输团队知道补救方案是否真的能送到客户手中。换一家供应商可能保住生产，却抬高到岸成本；把工作转到另一家工厂可能缓解一个瓶颈，却制造另一个瓶颈；加急发运成品可能保住交期——前提是仓库能在发运前备好货。每一种选择都会在别处改变问题。

这就是作者提出的 Planning Decision Fabric 要解决的业务挑战：把运营事实、业务政策、计算与问责行动连接起来，覆盖供应链全程。作者在早期的文章中描述了跨 Oracle E-Business Suite、ASCP、Fusion 供应链计划与更广泛企业的决策架构；这个系列把架构推进到实施层面，共 12 期，探索 Oracle 与 NVIDIA 技术如何支撑一个可实际运行的循环：感知—理解—评估—决策—执行—学习。

改进客户承诺是业务目标。采购决策决定物料何时到位，到位情况决定生产，生产决定仓库工作量，仓库就绪程度影响发运，运输选择影响交付表现、利润与下一次客户沟通。客户优先级、合同承诺、分配规则、替代、部分发运与服务成本都属于决策的一部分。一个有用的系统必须展示答案背后的证据：哪些假设重要、哪些约束在起作用、谁能批准应对措施、批准的行动是否真的执行了。

实施从已经运行业务的应用开始。EBS、ASCP、Fusion 与其他企业应用承载着多年的业务配置、计划逻辑、交易历史与运营控制。原生计划能力仍是核心；当决策跨越应用边界或提出额外要求时，专门的优化才有价值。每次交接都需要明确的约定：用的是哪个计划版本、包含哪些订单、什么可以移动、什么已冻结、批准的变更在哪里执行。组织可以在大型应用现代化项目继续推进的同时，先从一个有边界的决策流程起步。

可信的上下文把企业连接起来。智能体在推理缺料之前，系统必须先确定证据描述的是哪个物料、供应商地点、组织、订单、资源与日历，还必须确定时间：当天的供应商确认、昨天的库存与已被取代的生产计划，不能悄悄拼成同一幅'当前'图景。

技术上，Oracle AI Data Platform 为跨企业数据的治理与准备提供基础，Oracle Autonomous AI Lakehouse 以开放的多云方式把分析与 AI 能力带到分布式企业数据。Oracle AI Database 不只存放历史报表数据，还存放决策事实、依赖关系、场景版本、政策引用、建议与结果；关系、JSON、图、向量与空间能力按问题需要各司其职。Select AI 智能体框架支持贴近企业数据的智能体、工具、任务与团队；有边界的智能体可以评估场景输入是否足够新鲜、检索适用政策、组装证据或分析已保存的结果，每种角色都需要明确的权限、结构化输出与可观察的行为。计算与执行工具保持显性：有说服力的解释必须有有效的计算结果支撑。

NVIDIA 从几个方面扩展架构：cuOpt 提供路由与数学优化能力，用于有限产能排产、供应网络试验、仓库规划与运输整合；cuDF 加速表格数据的准备与分析；cuVS 支持 GPU 加速的相似检索；Nemotron 提供用于有依据的解释与工具型智能体工作流的模型选项；Omniverse 与 Isaac Sim 把旅程延伸到空间、物理、传感器与机器人验证。每个组件都须通过实测价值赢得位置：小规模工作负载用 SQL 或 CPU 处理可能已经足够好。

人员需要一个审视权衡的地方：计划员工作台。Oracle Analytics Cloud 支持共享业务度量与分析探索；Oracle APEX 提供调查例外、编辑场景、比较方案、复核证据与批准应对的操作体验。Open Agent Specification 以声明方式定义智能体与工作流，其与 AG-UI 的集成把兼容的智能体运行时连接到交互式应用体验；应用必须保留工具事件、场景引用、审批与执行结果，形成持久的审计轨迹。

12 期的实施旅程依次覆盖：从架构到可执行决策、构建供应链决策孪生、创建计划员工作台、调查与解释的智能体、连接销售链、优化供应响应、让工厂排产可行、把仓库当作受约束的运营来计划、优化交付与运输、大规模不确定性下的重新计划、连接决策与物理世界、跨企业应用闭环。实施示例会区分已验证的能力、历史重放、场景假设与拟议扩展；以 EBS 为基础的工作提供初始参考，Fusion 与其他应用模式将在明确的集成边界下展开。

原文

A supplier shipment slips by four days. For Maya, a supply chain planner, the first question is straightforward: which customer commitments are now at risk?

The answer is scattered across the enterprise. Purchasing holds the supplier's latest confirmation. Planning holds the expected supply. Manufacturing knows which jobs need the component. Customer operations understands which commitments matter most. Warehouse and transport teams know whether a recovery plan can actually reach the customer.

An alternate supplier might protect production but increase landed cost. Moving work to another plant might relieve one bottleneck and create another. Expediting finished goods might recover the delivery date, provided the warehouse can prepare the shipment before departure. Every option changes the problem somewhere else.

This is the business challenge behind the Planning Decision Fabric: connecting operational facts, business policies, calculations, and accountable action across the supply chain.

In my earlier article, I described an architecture for making those decisions across Oracle E-Business Suite, ASCP, Fusion Supply Planning, and the wider enterprise. This next series takes that architecture into implementation. Over twelve installments, I will explore how Oracle and NVIDIA technologies can support a practical operating loop: Sense, understand, evaluate, decide, execute, learn.

The starting point is my implementation work across supply chain planning, manufacturing scheduling, order fulfillment, warehouse operations, and logistics. The destination is a repeatable approach that organizations can adapt to their applications, infrastructure, and operating priorities.

The business objective is a better customer promise, and the ability to fulfill it. Supply chain performance depends on connected decisions. A sourcing decision determines when material becomes available. That availability shapes production. Production determines warehouse workload. Warehouse readiness influences dispatch. Transport choices affect delivery performance, margin, and the next customer conversation.

An improved production plan protects a customer promise only when warehouse and transport capacity can support it. Picking, packing, staging, loading, and delivery must therefore be evaluated alongside production, with service, cost, and risk considered together.

The commercial side belongs inside this loop. Customer priority, contractual commitments, allocation rules, substitutions, partial shipments, and cost to serve all influence the right supply response.

Maya's question therefore grows beyond identifying shortages: Which response protects the most important commitments, remains operationally feasible, and makes an acceptable business tradeoff?

A useful system must show the evidence behind its answer. It must explain which assumptions matter, what constraints are binding, who can approve the response, and whether the approved action actually happened. That is the standard this series will work toward.

Start with the applications that already run the business. EBS, ASCP, Fusion, and other enterprise applications contain years of business configuration, planning logic, transaction history, and operational controls. The implementation approach begins by identifying what each application owns and which engine should calculate each result.

Native planning capabilities remain central. Specialized optimization becomes useful when a decision crosses application boundaries or introduces requirements that justify an additional model: detailed resource constraints, warehouse staging limits, cross-network allocation, or repeated recovery experiments.

Each handoff needs a clear contract. What plan version are we using? Which orders are included? What can move? What is frozen? Where will an approved change be executed? Those questions make the architecture usable.

Start improving decisions before the next major upgrade. Organizations can begin with a bounded decision workflow while larger application modernization programs continue. This series will demonstrate incremental extensions, identifying what is implemented, what requires integration, and what remains an experiment.

Trusted context connects the enterprise. Teams often export planning results and manually reconcile them with customer priorities, execution conditions, and logistics constraints. A decision layer can connect this work through shared evidence, explicit policies, and repeatable scenario evaluation. The series will measure its value against a comparable baseline.

Before an agent can reason about a shortage, the system must establish which item, supplier site, organization, order, resource, and calendar the evidence describes. It must also establish time. A supplier confirmation from this morning, inventory from yesterday, and a superseded production plan cannot silently become one current picture.

The technology: Oracle AI Data Platform provides a foundation for governing and preparing cross-enterprise data. Oracle Autonomous AI Lakehouse supports the broader goal of bringing analytical and AI capabilities to distributed enterprise data through an open, multicloud approach.

For this series, deployment flexibility is a design objective: preserve the decision model and its contracts across supported environments, while selecting services and integrations appropriate to each location. Individual capabilities and deployment options still need to be matched to the target environment.

The result is a decision twin: the coherent set of facts, relationships, policies, and scenarios needed to evaluate a particular decision.

Oracle AI Database has an active role in the decision process. The database holds more than historical reporting data. In this architecture, Oracle AI Database provides a home for decision facts, dependencies, scenario versions, policy references, recommendations, and outcomes. Relational, JSON, graph, vector, and spatial capabilities can contribute according to the question being answered.

Select AI agents are already part of my implementation approach. Their importance extends beyond asking questions in natural language: the Select AI Agent framework supports agents, tools, tasks, and teams operating close to enterprise data.

The series will also explore additional database agents as the implementation develops. For example, a bounded agent could assess whether a scenario has sufficiently fresh inputs, retrieve applicable policies, assemble evidence, or analyze saved results. Each role needs defined permissions, structured outputs, and observable behavior.

The calculation and execution tools remain explicit. A persuasive explanation must be supported by a valid result.

NVIDIA extends the architecture in several distinct ways. Optimization is an immediate opportunity. NVIDIA cuOpt provides routing and mathematical optimization capabilities that can be applied through purpose-built models. In this project, that includes finite-capacity production scheduling, supply network experiments, warehouse planning, and transport consolidation. The quality of the formulation matters as much as the solver. Material balance, resource calendars, operation precedence, handling restrictions, service objectives, and frozen commitments must be represented correctly.

Other NVIDIA technologies address different needs: cuDF can accelerate suitable tabular preparation and analytical workloads as data volumes grow. cuVS can support GPU-accelerated similarity search where retrieval scale or latency warrants it. Nemotron provides model options for grounded interpretation, explanation, and tool-oriented agent workflows. Omniverse, with tools such as Isaac Sim where appropriate, extends the journey into spatial, physical, sensor, and robotics validation.

Each component should earn its place through measured usefulness. Small workloads may already perform well with SQL or CPU processing. Additional acceleration becomes valuable when it improves the operating decision within an acceptable time and cost.

People need a place to examine the tradeoff. The architecture becomes tangible through the planner's workbench. Oracle Analytics Cloud can support shared business measures and analytical exploration. Oracle APEX can provide operational experiences for investigating an exception, editing a scenario, comparing alternatives, reviewing evidence, and approving a response. APEXlang contributes to the project's approach for building and maintaining those applications through explicit specifications and validation.

Connecting agent workflows to the user experience: Open Agent Specification (Agent Spec) provides declarative definitions of agents and workflows. Its integration with AG-UI connects compatible agent runtimes to interactive application experiences. The application must retain tool events, scenario references, approvals, and execution results to create a durable audit trail.

Oracle Analytics Cloud AI agents add a complementary analytical role: helping users interpret service measures consistently and investigate performance using business data, tailored instructions, and supporting knowledge. Optimization and execution remain assigned to explicitly defined tools.

A planner should be able to move from an enterprise measure to an affected customer order, inspect its dependencies, compare feasible responses, and understand the consequence of a choice. The same scenario reference should connect the dashboard, agent conversation, solver result, approval, and execution record.

Physical AI becomes relevant when physical behavior changes feasibility. A schedule can respect capacity and still depend on unrealistic movement assumptions. A shipment can pass a weight-and-volume check while leaving physical fit unresolved. A warehouse plan can miss congestion around staging lanes or loading areas.

The series will explore a progression from schedule playback to operational simulation and, where justified, physical validation. The project already contains production and logistics playback experiences. More demanding physical AI applications require additional evidence: measured geometry, calibrated behavior, equipment characteristics, sensor models, and validation against real operations.

This creates a useful feedback loop. Physical experiments can reveal constraints that improve the planning model; revised plans can then be tested again before operational adoption.

The twelve-part implementation journey: Each installment will combine a business decision, the governing ideas, a working example, and an implementation companion. 1. From Architecture to an Executable Decision: Define the first use case, its decision contract, baseline, and measurable outcome. 2. Building the Supply Chain Decision Twin: Connect sources through consistent identity, time, relationships, and provenance. 3. Creating the Planner's Workbench: Bring analytics, evidence, scenarios, and review together with OAC, APEX, and APEXlang. 4. Agents That Investigate and Explain: Apply Select AI agents, Oracle agent capabilities, and Nemotron through bounded tools. 5. Connecting the Sell Chain: Evaluate customer commitments, scarce-supply allocation, service policies, and margin. 6. Optimizing the Supply Response: Compare sourcing, production, transfers, and logistics across the network. 7. Making the Factory Schedule Feasible: Connect planning to resources, calendars, material readiness, and frozen operations. 8. Planning the Warehouse as a Constrained Operation: Coordinate labor, skills, equipment, staging, and loading. 9. Optimizing Delivery and Transport: Evaluate consolidation, departure timing, vehicle capacity, and customer service. 10. Replanning at Scale Under Uncertainty: Benchmark acceleration and test how alternatives behave when assumptions vary. 11. Connecting Decisions to the Physical World: Explore Omniverse, physical validation, and relevant physical AI applications. 12. Closing the Loop Across Enterprise Applications: Govern approval, execution, reconciliation, monitoring, and learning.

The implementation examples will distinguish demonstrated capabilities, historical replays, scenario assumptions, and proposed extensions. The EBS-backed work provides an initial reference; Fusion and other application patterns will be developed with explicit integration boundaries.

Readers should be able to follow the complete journey or adopt one bounded capability that addresses an immediate operating problem.

When the supplier shipment slips again, Maya should have a shorter path from uncertainty to an accountable response: current evidence, feasible alternatives, visible tradeoffs, and a controlled way to act.

The next article begins with that first working decision.

This series reflects my own implementation work and views. Examples will identify their data, assumptions, and deployment scope.
