Cl0p 利用 Oracle E-Business Suite 漏洞持续攻击：NHS 被点名，华盛顿邮报确认受影响

勒索组织 Cl0p 于 2026 年 11 月 11 日在暗网泄露站点发布消息，声称已入侵英国国家医疗服务体系（NHS），指责该机构忽视客户安全。截至报道时，NHS 方面尚未确认发生入侵，该组织也未公布窃取数据的规模。NHS 网络安全部门曾在 10 月就 Oracle E-Business Suite 的严重缺陷发布警报，要求依赖该企业软件的医疗与公共部门系统立即打补丁并限制互联网暴露面；Cl0p 此次点名的时间点与该警报吻合，显示攻击可能利用了同一批缺陷。

同一攻击活动此前已波及华盛顿邮报：Cl0p 于 2026 年 11 月 7 日宣布得手，并发布据称 183GB 的数据，文件夹标注为 ebs.washpost.com。华盛顿邮报随后确认受到 Oracle E-Business Suite 平台入侵事件影响；2026 年 11 月 11 日向缅因州总检察长办公室提交的文件确认 9,720 名用户受影响，其个人与财务信息被暴露。

安全专家指出，此次攻击符合 Cl0p 大规模数据窃取的一贯模式，目标是多行业通用的企业软件。Outpost24 高级威胁情报分析师 Lidia Lopez 表示，该组织已从传统勒索加密转向利用关键软件零日缺陷发起协调一致的数据外泄行动，目标包括 MOVEit、GoAnywhere 与 Oracle E-Business Suite。与附属制勒索团伙不同，Cl0p 采用集中化、技术驱动的结构，可在厂商发布补丁前对数百家机构同步发起攻击；其战术通常是扫描存在缺陷的系统、获取远程访问、保持驻留，并在提出要求或公布泄露前数月悄悄窃取数据。

SOCRadar 技术内容经理 Faik Emre Derin 补充说，此次 Oracle E-Business Suite 攻击活动围绕 CVE-2025-61882 展开，这是一个 CVSS 9.8 分的严重远程代码执行缺陷。利用活动早在 2025 年 8 月已经开始，Oracle 于 2025 年 10 月 4 日发布紧急补丁；受影响版本为 12.2.3 至 12.2.14，攻击者集中利用允许未授权访问的 BI Publisher 集成模块。2025 年 10 月 3 日，另一组织 Scattered Lapsus$ Hunters 泄露了概念验证代码，此后攻击扩散加速，Cl0p 与 FIN11 等组织纷纷扩大攻击范围。Derin 建议运行 Oracle E-Business Suite 的组织立即安装 2025 年 10 月的补丁，追溯至 8 月的取证检查，并监控与 200.107.207.26、185.181.60.11 等可疑 IP 的连接。

此次攻击活动还波及哈佛大学与美国航空子公司 Envoy 等机构。Mandiant 与谷歌威胁情报团队的调查显示，攻击活动始于 2025 年 9 月下旬，目标是依赖 Oracle E-Business Suite 处理财务、人力与供应链业务的公司。Oracle 此后已发布修复补丁并敦促客户尽快更新，但仍有大量系统处于暴露状态，Cl0p 及关联组织仍有继续利用的机会。专家警告，随着被盗数据已在网上流传、更多受害者可能陆续浮现，未修复 Oracle 系统构成的威胁远未结束。

原文

Cl0p is claiming responsibility for a new data breach affecting the National Health Service (NHS UK). On November 11, 2026, the ransomware group posted on its dark web leak site, accusing the healthcare provider of neglecting its security, stating, “The company doesn’t care about its customers; it ignored their security.”

Although the group has not revealed the volume of stolen data, the announcement aligns with ongoing attacks and reports pointing out Cl0p of exploiting vulnerabilities in Oracle’s E-Business Suite (EBS).

NHS officials have not confirmed a breach, but their cybersecurity division did issue alerts in October about critical flaws in Oracle EBS. That alert warned healthcare and public sector systems relying on Oracle’s enterprise software to apply immediate patches and restrict internet exposure. The timing of the Cl0p post now suggests the group may have exploited the same vulnerabilities NHS had already flagged a month earlier.

While the NHS claim is still being investigated, Cl0p’s campaign has already proven its reach. Just days earlier, on November 7, the group announced it had breached The Washington Post by exploiting the same Oracle EBS flaws. As seen by Hackread.com, the hackers published what they claim is 183GB of data under a folder labeled `ebs.washpost.com`. The Washington Post later confirmed it was impacted, saying it was among the victims of a “breach of the Oracle E-Business Suite platform.” A filing with the Maine Attorney General’s Office on November 11 confirmed that The Post was affected, stating 9,720 users were affected and their personal and financial details were exposed.

Security experts say the Washington Post attack fits Cl0p’s pattern of large-scale, data-theft operations targeting enterprise software used across multiple sectors. Lidia Lopez, Senior Threat Intelligence Analyst at Outpost24, noted that the incident highlights Cl0p’s focus on exploiting high-value business systems rather than random targets. She explained that the group has moved away from traditional ransomware encryption toward coordinated data-exfiltration campaigns that leverage zero-day flaws in critical software such as MOVEit, GoAnywhere, and Oracle EBS.

Unlike affiliate-based ransomware operations, Cl0p’s structure is centralised and technical. Lopez said this setup allows the group to conduct synchronised attacks against hundreds of organisations before vendors release patches. Their tactics often involve scanning for vulnerable systems, gaining remote access, maintaining persistence, and quietly siphoning data for months before making demands or publishing leaks.

Faik Emre Derin, Technical Content Manager at SOCRadar, added that the Oracle EBS campaign revolves around CVE-2025-61882, a severe remote code execution flaw with a CVSS score of 9.8. His team’s analysis shows the exploitation began around August 2025, months before Oracle issued an emergency patch on October 4. The affected versions range from 12.2.3 to 12.2.14, with attackers focusing on the BI Publisher Integration module that allows unauthenticated access to vulnerable systems.

Derin said the exploit’s spread accelerated when a separate group called Scattered Lapsus$ Hunters leaked proof-of-concept code on October 3. This leak allowed additional threat actors, including Cl0p and FIN11, to expand attacks on a global scale. He recommended that organisations running Oracle EBS install the October 2025 patch immediately, conduct forensic reviews dating back to August, and monitor for connections to suspicious IPs such as 200.107.207.26 and 185.181.60.11.

The ongoing campaign has also affected other high-profile organisations, including Harvard University and American Airlines’ subsidiary Envoy. Investigations by Mandiant and Google’s Threat Intelligence Group suggest the activity started in late September 2025, targeting companies that rely heavily on Oracle EBS for finance, HR, and supply-chain management.

Oracle has since released patches addressing the vulnerabilities and urged customers to update without delay. Despite those efforts, many systems remain exposed, providing Cl0p and affiliated groups with continued opportunities for exploitation.

The inclusion of NHS UK and The Washington Post in the victim list places the Cl0p campaign among the most significant enterprise-software breaches in recent years. With stolen data already circulating online and more victims expected to surface, experts warn that the threat from unpatched Oracle systems is far from over.
