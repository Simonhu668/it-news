Your Spoken Half

Your hand learned the drill before your mouth did. On the night of October 19, you wrote, unprompted: "ecn的IT价值不在机房维护和维修电脑，而在保证业务部门的业务系统持续稳定运行."

I checked it against the drill's example from two nights earlier — "IT 的价值不在机房里，而在业务部门不用再为系统操心的那一天." The skeleton is identical, but not one word is borrowed. That was the drill living in your hand, unprompted, doing real work.

The next night's voice message told the other half of the story. You were replaying the Oracle master data meeting, and the speech was the old load: looping thoughts, 嗯 and 呃 stacking up, sentences restarting halfway. I don't say this to grade you — I wasn't counting, and I wouldn't. I say it because the contrast is the whole diagnosis: the drill trained your writing, because the drills are reading and writing. Your mouth never got the same reps.

Your hand learned the drill before your mouth did.

With Ralph arriving November 9, this matters, because the 1:1s won't be a writing exercise. They'll be spoken, live, unscripted — exactly the conditions of that voice message. But here's the genuinely good news: you don't need new material. You already own the two lines that will do the heaviest lifting in that room. The IT-value line you wrote yourself, and the 知识扁平化 framing we built the other night — the one where the meeting stops being a 责任划分大会 and data problems get caught 在源头 instead of blowing up downstream.

So the one move I'd make this week is small: read each line aloud twice, then keep talking around it for thirty unscripted seconds, then count the fillers the way the drill already does. Ten minutes, once. Your hand built the first half of the skill. Saying the lines builds the second.
